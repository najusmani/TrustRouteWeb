Launching a wallet programme: the decisions that matter most

A successful mobile wallet is a business, not just a product. The decisions on proposition, regulation, funds, agents, merchants and operations.

Wallets9 November 20263 min readBy TrustRoute Technologies

Key takeaways

  • Start with a clear customer problem, not with features.
  • Settle regulation and how customer funds are safeguarded early.
  • Cash-in, cash-out and merchant acceptance make or break adoption.
  • Budget for operations: support, fraud and reconciliation never stop.

Launching a mobile wallet is easy to underestimate. The technology is the visible part; the decisions that determine success sit around it, in regulation, distribution and day-to-day operations.

Start with the proposition

Be precise about who the wallet is for and what problem it solves. Paying salaries to workers without bank accounts, collecting payments for small merchants, sending money home and paying bills are very different propositions, with different users, fees and partners. A clear proposition shapes every later decision.

Regulation and licensing

Wallet programmes are regulated differently in each market. Some regulators require a dedicated licence; in others, a bank can offer a wallet under its existing licence, or a provider can operate with a licensed partner bank. Engage your regulator early and settle questions such as customer due diligence, account tiers and transaction limits before you design the product.

How customer funds are held

Customers must be able to trust that their money is safe. Decide how wallet balances are held and safeguarded, how the wallet ledger reconciles with those funds every day, and how settlement with partners works. This design is fundamental and difficult to change later.

Cash-in, cash-out and agents

For many customers, a wallet is only useful if they can easily put cash in and take it out. That often means an agent network: shops and outlets where customers can deposit and withdraw. Recruiting, training, supplying and monitoring agents is a business in its own right, and it frequently decides whether a wallet is adopted.

Somewhere to spend it

A wallet needs places to use it. Plan merchant acceptance from the start: QR payments, bill payment partners, and transfers to bank accounts and other wallets. Interoperability with other providers in your market can significantly increase usefulness.

Onboarding and limits

Tiered accounts let customers start quickly with simple checks and low limits, and move to higher limits as they provide more information. Make onboarding fast and inclusive, while matching each tier's limits to its level of verification.

Operations never stop

  • Customer support for forgotten PINs, failed transfers and disputes.
  • Fraud monitoring tuned to wallet behaviour, including agent fraud.
  • Daily reconciliation between the wallet ledger, partners and safeguarded funds.
  • Regulatory reporting on time, every time.

Reach every customer

Not every customer has a smartphone. Offering wallet services over USSD or SMS, alongside an app, can widen reach considerably, especially in the early stages of adoption.

Launch, measure, improve

Start with a focused launch, measure active use rather than registrations, and listen closely to agents and customers. The wallets that succeed are the ones that keep improving after launch day.
