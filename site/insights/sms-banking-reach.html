Why SMS banking still reaches customers that apps cannot

Mobile apps get the attention, but SMS and USSD still reach every phone. Where SMS banking fits, how to keep it secure, and how to design it well.

Channels9 November 20263 min readBy TrustRoute Technologies

Key takeaways

  • Not every customer can, or wants to, use a banking app.
  • SMS and USSD work on any phone, without data or downloads.
  • Use SMS for what it does well, and be clear about its security limits.
  • Plan channels together: SMS, WhatsApp and apps can share one integration.

Banks invest heavily in mobile apps, and rightly so. Yet a meaningful share of customers will never download one. For them, a simple text message is still the most reliable way to bank.

The app gap

Apps assume a smartphone, enough storage, a data connection and the confidence to install and trust software. Many customers lack at least one of these: people in rural areas with patchy coverage, older customers who prefer simple tools, small businesses using a basic handset, and anyone who simply does not want another app. These customers still need to know their balance, receive alerts and move money.

What SMS and USSD do well

SMS and USSD work on every mobile phone, over the basic mobile network, without data or downloads. They are familiar, fast and resilient: when data networks are congested or an app is unavailable, a text message usually still gets through. That makes them well suited to:

  • Balance and mini-statement enquiries answered in seconds.
  • Transaction alerts that help customers spot problems early.
  • Simple transfers and payments to known beneficiaries, within sensible limits.
  • Service continuity when other channels are down.

Be clear about security

SMS was not designed as a secure channel, and a good SMS banking service is honest about that. Protect customers with a PIN on every transaction, sensible transaction limits, and controls against SIM-swap fraud, such as checking for recent SIM changes before allowing sensitive actions. Never send full account numbers or other sensitive data in a message. For one-time passcodes, SMS remains widely used, but banks should offer stronger options where customers can use them.

Design for the channel

SMS banking succeeds when it is simple. Keep commands short and memorable, confirm every transaction clearly, and support the languages your customers actually use. USSD menus should be shallow, with the most common tasks one or two steps away. Test with real customers on basic handsets, not just with staff on smartphones.

Not either-or

SMS banking is not a rival to your app; it is part of the same channel strategy. A well-designed integration with your core banking system can serve SMS, USSD, WhatsApp and the app from one set of services, so new features reach every channel without being built several times.

Measure what matters

Track active users, the most-used commands, failed requests and the share of customers for whom SMS is their only digital channel. Those numbers show the real reach of the service, and often make a stronger case for it than any assumption about how customers ought to bank.
